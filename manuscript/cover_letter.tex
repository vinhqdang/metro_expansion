Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript ``Fair and Transferable Graph Reinforcement Learning for
Urban Rail Transit Network Expansion Across Asian Cities'' for consideration as a
regular research article in \emph{Computational Economics}.

The underlying decision problem is an economic one before it is an engineering one: a
city choosing where to extend its rail network is making a large, essentially
irreversible public-investment allocation under a fixed station/budget constraint, and
must set a trade-off between aggregate transportation-demand efficiency and equitable
access to new infrastructure across neighborhoods that differ in income and existing
service. That trade-off is normally fixed once, by policy, before a network is built. We
formulate it instead as a sequential allocation problem solved by reinforcement learning
over a graph-structured representation of the city's regions and travel demand, with a
Tchebycheff-scalarized vector objective (demand satisfaction, social equity, radiation
accessibility) that lets a single trained policy be queried at different points on the
efficiency-equity frontier rather than committing to one weighting in advance -- an
agent-based, machine-learning approach to a resource-allocation and welfare
trade-off problem, which is the class of question this journal's scope names directly.

The paper's contribution is threefold: a graph neural network encoder over the city's
spatial-contiguity and origin-destination relations, feeding a multi-objective
actor-critic policy that extends prior single-objective (MetroGNN) and
non-GNN multi-objective (Zhang et al. 2024) formulations of the metro network expansion
problem; a cross-city curriculum-transfer mechanism, motivated directly by a recent
critique (Michailidis et al. 2026) that deep RL's training cost is unjustified at this
problem's scale, that pretrains on one city's network and warm-starts another's to test
whether transfer buys back that cost in sample efficiency; and, to our knowledge, the
first evaluation of this problem class spanning both a small, data-sparse emerging
network (Ho Chi Minh City) and a mature, previously-studied one (Xi'an) in one
consistent protocol.

The empirical results are reported as found rather than smoothed toward the hoped-for
conclusion. A five-seed sweep on Xi'an finds no reliable advantage for the full method
over either architectural ablation on demand, equity, or coverage, and the tabular RL
baseline of Michailidis et al. still wins on raw demand under a matched training
budget -- a negative result for model complexity that we think is itself of interest to
a computational-economics readership asking when a more expressive (and more expensive)
representation is worth its cost. Tracing an earlier, more favorable-looking equity
finding, we discovered GPU training non-determinism severe enough to invert which
architecture a metric appears to favor, traced it to two distinct causes, fixed both, and
verified deterministic re-runs -- a reproducibility hazard we believe generalizes beyond
our own implementation to other GNN-based methods built on the same default
scatter-reduction operations. On Ho Chi Minh City, an encoder ablation collapses to
near-zero demand where the full method does not, and warm-starting from Xi'an's trained
policy shows genuine positive transfer at initialization and, under a reduced training
budget, reaches non-trivial demand and consistently higher coverage than training from
scratch -- direct evidence for the curriculum's sample-efficiency claim on the one city
where we could test it. A separately implemented radiation-model accessibility measure
does not share the coverage proxy's architectural sensitivity, a construct-validity
finding we report rather than omit.

All code -- the GNN encoder, multi-objective policy, training and evaluation scripts for
both cities, and the full test suite -- is publicly available now, not embargoed until
publication, at \url{https://github.com/vinhqdang/metro_expansion}. Data sources are
public third-party releases (Michailidis et al.'s \texttt{mo-tndp}, the CityScope/MIT
\texttt{CSL\_HCMC} repository, and \texttt{GZUPA/subway-traffic-data-set}), cited and
documented in the manuscript's Data Availability statement.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. I am the sole author and approve its
submission. I declare no competing financial or personal interests, and the work
received no specific grant from any funding agency. In accordance with the journal's
policy, the manuscript will include a declaration of generative-AI use in its
preparation.
