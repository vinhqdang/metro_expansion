TITLE
Fairness-aware multi-objective metro line extension: a matched-budget comparison of learning-based and evolutionary planning

ABSTRACT
Learned construction policies are proposed for metro network expansion, but they are rarely compared with evolutionary multi-objective search at an equal simulation budget. We compare a weight-conditioned graph reinforcement learning policy with NSGA-II, scalarised genetic algorithms, tabular Q-learning, a greedy rule and random search on the Xi'an and Amsterdam benchmarks, with about 10,000 simulated lines per method and ten seeds. We replace the benchmark's summed percentage reward by the true demand share, score fairness by a generalised Gini welfare, and measure accessibility as newly reached trip-end mass. On Xi'an the search archive of the learned policy is comparable with that of NSGA-II (hypervolume 0.753 against 0.773, p = 0.064), but its 15 deployable lines are worse (expected utility -0.166 against -0.106, p = 0.006). On Amsterdam NSGA-II is better on both measures. The policy returns nearly the same line for different weights. In a leave-one-line-out test, NSGA-II recovers the real Xi'an Line 2 about twice as well as random search, and the policy does not differ from random. A warm start helped from Amsterdam to Xi'an (5 of 5 seeds) but not the reverse; five pairs cannot establish either effect. At equal budget, NSGA-II is the stronger choice.

KEYWORDS
metro network expansion; reinforcement learning; multi-objective optimisation; evolutionary algorithms; NSGA-II; equity; Pareto front
