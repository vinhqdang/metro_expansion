TITLE
Fairness-aware multi-objective metro line extension: a matched-budget comparison of learning-based and evolutionary planning

ABSTRACT
Learned construction policies are proposed for metro network expansion, but they are rarely compared with evolutionary multi-objective search at an equal simulation budget, or scored on every objective. We compare a weight-conditioned graph reinforcement learning policy with NSGA-II, scalarised genetic algorithms, tabular Q-learning, a greedy rule and random search on the public Xi'an and Amsterdam benchmark grids, with about 10,000 simulated lines per method and ten seeds. We replace the benchmark's summed percentage reward by the true share of demand served, score fairness by a generalised Gini welfare that is zero when nobody is served, and measure accessibility as the trip-end mass newly reached. On Xi'an, the search archive of the learned policy is comparable with that of NSGA-II (hypervolume 0.753 against 0.773, p = 0.064), but its 15 deployable lines are worse (expected utility -0.166 against -0.106, p = 0.006). On the larger Amsterdam grid NSGA-II is better on both measures. The policy produces nearly the same line for different weights. In a leave-one-line-out test on Xi'an, NSGA-II recovers the real Line 2 about twice as well as a random-search baseline, and the learned policy does not differ from random. A warm start from another city helped in one direction (Amsterdam to Xi'an, 5 of 5 seeds) and not in the other, and five pairs are too few to establish either effect. For this benchmark, NSGA-II is the stronger choice at equal budget; the learned policy is not yet a replacement.

KEYWORDS
metro network expansion; reinforcement learning; multi-objective optimisation; evolutionary algorithms; NSGA-II; equity; Pareto front
