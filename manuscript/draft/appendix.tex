\section{Reproducibility details}\label{app:repro}
All results come from run records written by the scripts in the repository, and every number in the text and tables is generated from those records. Training and search are deterministic given the seed: PyTorch runs single-threaded with deterministic algorithms enabled, the start cell is chosen by the policy, and random numbers come from seeded generators. The fast environment used for all methods reproduces the upstream environment's rewards to within $10^{-12}$ on 300 seeded random episodes per setting (Xi'an, Amsterdam, and the Line-1-only instance, with and without chained reward). The genetic algorithm and tabular agent of \citet{michailidis2026} were run from their public code; the method that evaluates the learned Q-table reads the agent's position from the environment info, as their training loop does, and one array-to-integer conversion in their mutation operator was adjusted for the installed numerical library. About 41\% of their genetic offspring are infeasible and are dropped, so the algorithm completes about 5\,100 lines in 10\,100 attempts.

\section{Hyperparameters}\label{app:hyper}
The policy has 32-dimensional embeddings, two message-passing layers, Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$ decaying to $10^{-4}$ by a cosine schedule, gradient clipping at 1.0, 16 episodes per update (4 weights $\times$ 4 rollouts), entropy weight 0.01 decaying linearly over 70\% of the updates, prior strength 1 for both heads, and 10\,000 episodes. These were chosen among six configurations on the tuning seeds by the mean expected utility of the greedy 15-point front. Without the prior, the best of eight configurations used entropy weight 0.1. NSGA-II uses population 50 and 201 generations, chosen among six splits of the same total budget. The scalarised genetic algorithm uses population 30 and 21 generations per weight vector. The evaluation seeds are 0 to 9 and the tuning seeds are 100 to 102.
