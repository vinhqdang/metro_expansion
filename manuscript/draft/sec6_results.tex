\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Xi'an: matched-budget comparison}\label{sec:res-xian}
Table~\ref{tab:main_xian_full} compares all methods on the Xi'an instance with ten evaluation seeds. The two primary endpoints of the analysis plan are the hypervolume of the search archive and the expected utility of the deployable front, both for the learned policy against NSGA-II.

\input{generated/tab_main_xian_full.tex}

\paragraph{Search quality.} The archive of the RL runs contains every line sampled during training. Its hypervolume is \XianRLArchHv\ ($\pm\,\XianRLArchHvSd$), against \XianNSGAArchHv\ ($\pm\,\XianNSGAArchHvSd$) for NSGA-II. The difference is \XianRLvsNSGAArchHvDiff\ (95\% interval \XianRLvsNSGAArchHvDiffLo\ to \XianRLvsNSGAArchHvDiffHi, $A_{12}=\XianRLvsNSGAArchHvVda$, Holm-adjusted $p=\XianRLvsNSGAArchHvPHolm$), so we cannot distinguish the two. The RL archive is better than that of the matched scalarised GA ($p=\XianRLvsGAMatchedArchHvPHolm$) and far better than those of the two upstream baselines, tabular Q-learning and random lines (all $p\le\XianRLvsTabDemandArchHvPHolm$). By this measure the sampling distribution that the policy learns explores the trade-off surface about as well as NSGA-II does.

\paragraph{Deployable solutions.} The picture changes when we look at what each method hands to a planner for the 15 weight vectors. The expected utility of the RL policy is \XianRLEum\ ($\pm\,\XianRLEumSd$), against \XianNSGAEum\ ($\pm\,\XianNSGAEumSd$) for NSGA-II and \XianGAMatchedEum\ for the matched scalarised GA. NSGA-II is better than the RL policy by \XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumDiff\ in expected utility (95\% interval \XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumDiffLo\ to \XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumDiffHi, $A_{12}=\XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumVda$, $p=\XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumPHolm$), and the matched scalarised GA is better too ($p=\XianRLvsGAMatchedPolicyEumPHolm$). The RL policy is better than random lines ($p=\XianRLvsRandomPolicyEumPHolm$) and than the single-line baselines. Under the analysis plan this outcome is a loss for the learned policy on the second primary endpoint, and we state it as such.

\paragraph{Reading the baselines.} The genetic algorithm and the tabular agent of \citet{michailidis2026} return one line optimised for a single reward, so they cannot cover a trade-off set and score below random lines on expected utility. This follows from their design for a single objective. The greedy baseline, which picks the best one-step move at every station, is worse still. Greedy lines stall under the straight-line constraint, which shows that the one-step features given to the policy are not enough on their own.

\paragraph{Reference.} A method that also picks the best of 15 candidate lines by $g$ gains from the selection alone. A random method with this rule reaches an F1 within one cell of \HoldoutRandomVertexFOne\ ($\pm\,\HoldoutRandomVertexFOneSd$) at the demand vertex and \HoldoutRandomBalancedFOne\ ($\pm\,\HoldoutRandomBalancedFOneSd$) at the balanced weight. We compare the methods with this reference, using Mann--Whitney tests with Holm correction over the three compared methods, and not with single random lines. About \HoldoutNullDeadEnd\,\% of the \HoldoutNullN\ single random lines stop before 27 cells, which lowers their recall.

\paragraph{Result.} NSGA-II reaches an F1 of \HoldoutNSGAVertexFOne\ at the demand vertex ($A_{12}=\HoldoutVsRandomNSGAVertexA$ against the random reference, Holm-adjusted $p=\HoldoutVsRandomNSGAVertexPHolm$) and \HoldoutNSGABalancedFOne\ at the balanced weight ($p=\HoldoutVsRandomNSGABalancedPHolm$). The matched scalarised GA reaches \HoldoutGAMatchedBalancedFOne\ at the balanced weight ($p=\HoldoutVsRandomGAMatchedBalancedPHolm$). The RL policy reaches \HoldoutRLVertexFOne\ and \HoldoutRLBalancedFOne\ at the two settings, which we cannot distinguish from the random reference ($p=\HoldoutVsRandomRLVertexPHolm$ and $\HoldoutVsRandomRLBalancedPHolm$). The standard deviations over seeds are large, for example $\pm\,\HoldoutNSGABalancedFOneSd$ for NSGA-II, so these are statements about averages. The lines of these three methods serve more demand than the real Line~2 (balanced-weight ratios of \HoldoutNSGABalancedRatio\ for NSGA-II, \HoldoutGAMatchedBalancedRatio\ for the matched GA and \HoldoutRLBalancedRatio\ for RL), which is expected because the lines are optimised for the same OD matrix.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Under the objectives used here, evolutionary multi-objective search recovers part of the real alignment more often than chance, and the learned policy does not. The result is consistent with demand-driven planning of the corridor but is not evidence for it. The OD matrix was recorded while both lines were operating, so demand near Line~2 reflects its presence, and Line~2 opened in 2011, before Line~1 in 2013. A real alignment also reflects cost, geology, heritage and land acquisition, none of which the model sees.

\subsection{A larger instance: Amsterdam}\label{sec:res-amsterdam}
Amsterdam has $1\,645$ cells, about twice as many as Xi'an. Table~\ref{tab:main_amsterdam_full} gives the results with the same budgets and the same tuned configurations, without re-tuning. The OD edges of the graph were limited to the 64 strongest per cell to keep one encoder pass fast.

\input{generated/tab_main_amsterdam_full.tex}

The ranking is the same as on Xi'an, with larger gaps. The archive hypervolume of the RL runs is \AmsterdamRLArchHv, against \AmsterdamNSGAArchHv\ for NSGA-II. The difference of \AmsterdamRLvsNSGAArchHvDiff\ (95\% interval \AmsterdamRLvsNSGAArchHvDiffLo\ to \AmsterdamRLvsNSGAArchHvDiffHi, $A_{12}=\AmsterdamRLvsNSGAArchHvVda$, Holm-adjusted $p=\AmsterdamRLvsNSGAArchHvPHolm$) is now detectable, whereas on Xi'an it was not. The RL archive is as good as that of the matched scalarised GA ($p=\AmsterdamRLvsGAMatchedArchHvPHolm$). On the deployable front, NSGA-II is better by \AmsterdamRLvsNSGAPolicyEumDiff\ in expected utility ($A_{12}=\AmsterdamRLvsNSGAPolicyEumVda$, $p=\AmsterdamRLvsNSGAPolicyEumPHolm$), and the RL policy still beats random lines ($p=\AmsterdamRLvsRandomPolicyEumPHolm$). We therefore find no sign that the learned policy gains on the larger grid. The hypothesis that it would is not supported at this size. It remains untested for networks an order of magnitude larger.

\paragraph{The 27-station setting.} In the instance used for the line-holdout test, the lines are longer and the instance has only one existing line. The RL archive hypervolume there is \XianLineOneOnlyRLArchHv, against \XianLineOneOnlyNSGAArchHv\ for NSGA-II (difference \XianLineOneOnlyRLvsNSGAArchHvDiff, Holm-adjusted $p=\XianLineOneOnlyRLvsNSGAArchHvPHolm$). The learned policy is therefore also behind in this setting.

\paragraph{Cost.} One RL run takes about \XianRLWall\,s on Xi'an and \AmsterdamRLWall\,s on Amsterdam, against \XianNSGAWall\,s and \AmsterdamNSGAWall\,s for NSGA-II on one core. The RL runs also compute encoder passes and lookahead features, which the evolutionary methods do not.

\subsection{Ablations of the policy}\label{sec:res-ablation}
We remove one component at a time and run five seeds (0--4) of each variant on Xi'an. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares each variant with the full method on the same seeds. With five seeds per group the smallest exact two-sided Mann--Whitney $p$-value is 0.008, or 0.024 after Holm correction over three contrasts.

\input{generated/tab_ablation.tex}

\paragraph{Message passing.} Replacing the graph encoder by a linear projection lowers the expected utility from \AblFullEum\ to \AblNoMessagePassingEum\ (difference \AblNoMessagePassingEumDiff, Holm-adjusted $p=\AblNoMessagePassingEumPHolm$) and the archive hypervolume from \AblFullHv\ to \AblNoMessagePassingHv\ ($p=\AblNoMessagePassingHvPHolm$). This is the clearest effect among the ablations. The variant produces more distinct deployable lines (\AblNoMessagePassingLines) than the full method (\AblFullLines), so variety alone does not give quality.

\paragraph{Scalarised prior.} Without the fixed prior, the 15 deployable points collapse to one line (\AblNoPriorLines\ distinct lines on average) and the expected utility falls to \AblNoPriorEum\ ($p=\AblNoPriorEumPHolm$). The archive hypervolume is not clearly different ($p=\AblNoPriorHvPHolm$). The prior accounts for the weight sensitivity the policy has.

\paragraph{Weights.} Training with a fixed demand-only weight lowers the expected utility to \AblDemandOnlyEum\ ($p=\AblDemandOnlyEumPHolm$), as expected for a method judged on three objectives. Its archive hypervolume is lower by \AblDemandOnlyHvDiff\ but the difference is not detectable with five seeds ($p=\AblDemandOnlyHvPHolm$).

\subsection{Cross-city warm start}\label{sec:res-transfer}
We trained the policy on one city and used it to initialise training on the other, in both directions. The source policy of seed $s$ initialises the target run of seed $s$. The target run has 2\,000 episodes, one fifth of the full budget. Six conditions are compared: scratch, all transferable parameters (\emph{full}), the encoder only, the move and start heads only, all parameters with a frozen encoder, and a random frozen encoder. The input projection is transferred as well, since both cities use the same feature layout. Five seeds per cell is a small sample. With five pairs the smallest exact two-sided Wilcoxon $p$-value is 0.0625, so no contrast can reach $p<0.05$ in this study, with or without Holm correction. We report effect sizes and the number of seeds in which the warm start is better.

\input{generated/tab_transfer_amsterdam_to_xian.tex}
\input{generated/tab_transfer_xian_to_amsterdam.tex}

\paragraph{Amsterdam to Xi'an.} Warm-starting Xi'an from an Amsterdam policy gives an expected utility of \TrAmsXianFullEum, against \TrAmsXianScratchEum\ from scratch. The warm start is better in \TrAmsXianFullWins\ of 5 seeds, with a Hodges--Lehmann shift of \TrAmsXianFullHl\ (95\% interval \TrAmsXianFullHlLo\ to \TrAmsXianFullHlHi, exact $p=\TrAmsXianFullP$, Holm-adjusted $p=\TrAmsXianFullPHolm$). With 2\,000 episodes the warm-started policy comes close to the value of training from scratch for the full 10\,000 episodes (Table~\ref{tab:main_xian_full}). The component controls point the same way: the heads alone give \TrAmsXianHeadsDiff\ (better in \TrAmsXianHeadsWins\ of 5 seeds), the frozen transferred encoder gives \TrAmsXianFrozenTransferredDiff\ (\TrAmsXianFrozenTransferredWins\ of 5), and a frozen random encoder gives \TrAmsXianFrozenRandomDiff\ (\TrAmsXianFrozenRandomWins\ of 5). A frozen random encoder is not better than scratch, so training fewer parameters does not explain the gain.

\paragraph{Xi'an to Amsterdam.} In the other direction we see no benefit from the full warm start (\TrXianAmsFullDiff, better in \TrXianAmsFullWins\ of 5 seeds). Only the heads give a small and consistent gain (\TrXianAmsHeadsDiff, \TrXianAmsHeadsWins\ of 5), and freezing the encoder is worse than scratch.

\paragraph{Reading.} Transfer helps in one direction and not in the other. A policy trained on the larger and richer grid may carry more useful structure than one trained on the smaller grid, but with one city pair and five seeds we cannot separate that explanation from chance. We do not claim that the method transfers in general. The study also does not show an effect at the full training budget.
