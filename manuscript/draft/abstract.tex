Learned construction policies are proposed for metro network expansion, but they are rarely compared with evolutionary multi-objective search at an equal simulation budget. We compare a weight-conditioned graph reinforcement learning policy with NSGA-II, scalarised genetic algorithms, tabular Q-learning, a greedy rule and random search on the Xi'an and Amsterdam benchmarks, with about $10^4$ simulated lines per method and ten seeds. We replace the benchmark's summed percentage reward by the true demand share, score fairness by a generalised Gini welfare, and measure accessibility as newly reached trip-end mass. On Xi'an the search archive of the learned policy is comparable with that of NSGA-II (hypervolume \XianRLArchHv\ against \XianNSGAArchHv, $p=\XianRLvsNSGAArchHvPHolm$), but its 15 deployable lines are worse (expected utility \XianRLEum\ against \XianNSGAEum, $p=\XianRLvsNSGAPolicyEumPHolm$). On Amsterdam NSGA-II is better on both measures. The policy returns nearly the same line for different weights. In a leave-one-line-out test, NSGA-II recovers the real Xi'an Line~2 about twice as well as random search, and the policy does not differ from random. A warm start helped from Amsterdam to Xi'an (\TrAmsXianFullWins\ of 5 seeds) but not the reverse; five pairs cannot establish either effect. At equal budget, NSGA-II is the stronger choice.
